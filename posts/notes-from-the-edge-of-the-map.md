When I am stuck, my first instinct is usually to search harder. More tabs, more documentation, another tutorial that promises to make the shape of the problem obvious.

Sometimes that works. Often, the useful move is to stop looking for the answer and start drawing the map.

## Questions are better landmarks

At the edge of what I know, I write down the questions I can actually answer:

- What do I know for certain?
- What changed most recently?
- What would I expect to see if my assumption were true?
- What is the smallest experiment that could prove me wrong?

The questions turn a foggy problem into a set of small paths. Most of them end quickly. One of them usually leads somewhere.

## Stay curious, not certain

Good work is not always about arriving with the right answer. It is about building a habit of noticing when the answer you have is too convenient.

The edge of the map is a useful place to spend time. There, certainty gives way to attention — and attention is where the interesting work starts.
