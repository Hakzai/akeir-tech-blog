There is a particular kind of software I keep returning to: the kind that does one thing, does it well, and then gets out of the way.

The best tools become part of the shape of your day. You stop thinking about the interface and start thinking about the work. A good editor, a little script, a carefully designed command — they give you back a few seconds at a time until those seconds become room to think.

## Make the important path obvious

When a tool is small, every decision has weight. There is less room for a confusing menu or a feature that exists only because it can. The interface should point towards the useful thing:

1. Make the default action the right action.
2. Keep the feedback immediate and legible.
3. Let advanced behavior stay available without making it the main event.

This is not minimalism for its own sake. It is respect for someone else's attention.

> A tool is successful when it gives the user's attention back to them.

## The joy of a small surface

Small tools are also easier to understand, repair, and leave behind. That matters. Technology becomes more humane when we can see its edges — when we know what it does and what it does not do.

I am trying to make more things like that: quiet, dependable objects that help without asking to be the center of the room.
