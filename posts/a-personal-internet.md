The internet was once full of small rooms. Personal sites, hand-built pages, strange experiments, and writing that did not need to become a brand.

I miss some of that texture. Not because the old web was perfect, but because it made space for a different relationship with publishing. You could make a thing, put it somewhere, and let it exist without optimizing every sentence for an invisible score.

## Owning a small corner

This site is an attempt to build one of those rooms. It is deliberately simple: Markdown files, a small piece of JavaScript, and a design that tries to make reading feel calm.

There is no dashboard here. No algorithm deciding what deserves attention. When I have something to say, I write a file and publish it.

That pace feels good. It creates a little distance between having a thought and turning it into a post — enough distance to find out whether the thought has legs.

## Publishing slowly

Slow publishing is not the same as infrequent publishing. It means choosing care over velocity. It means leaving room for a note to be unfinished, or for an idea to change its mind.

The personal internet does not have to compete with the commercial one. It can simply be personal again.
